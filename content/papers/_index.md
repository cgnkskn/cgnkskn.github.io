#### Research Interests: Firm Dynamics, innovation, trade and production networks

## Working Papers

> <span style="color: #004488">**Firm Scope and Innovation: The Role of Intangibles (JMP)**</span>  
<span class="emoji-link">📑</span><span style="color: #0066cc">*[Latest (Version 2.4)](KESKIN_JMP.pdf)*</span> | </span> </span><span style="color: #0066cc">*[CERGE-EI WP Version](https://www.cerge-ei.cz/pdf/wp/Wp811.pdf)*</span> | <small>[**Best Paper Award**, The Econometric Society Asia Meeting](https://www.cerge-ei.cz/news/cerge-ei-researcher-cagin-keskin-wins-ames-csw-best-paper-award-2026)</small>

<details class="abstract-toggle">
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
  <div class="abstract-body">

Horizontal expansion through an increasing product portfolio lies at the core of modern endogenous growth literature. I document that diversified firms shift investment toward embedded intangibles (organizational capital, brand value) at the expense of transferable intangibles (patents, software). This strategic reallocation is accompanied by declining markups and productivity, and reduced innovation by rivals. In an extended endogenous-growth framework, embedded intangibles drive horizontal expansion but raise entry barriers and decrease social returns, ultimately sacrificing economy-wide growth for firm-level advantages.

  </div>
</details>

> <span style="color: #004488">**Capital Injection in the Production Network**</span>  
<span class="emoji-link">📝</span><span style="color: #0066cc">*[Draft PDF (Version 1.0)](Network_Acquisitions_Paper_September_2026.pdf)*</span> | Joint work with [Paolo Zacchia](https://www.paolozacchia.com/)

<details class="abstract-toggle">
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
  <div class="abstract-body">

Using Turkish administrative firm-to-firm transaction data, we study how acquisitions affect target firms and their production networks. We find that acquisitions increase intangible intensity but have no consistent effects on conventional performance measures. Network consequences depend on acquirer origin: domestic acquisitions strengthen existing relationships, while foreign acquisitions promote outsourcing and diversification. We attribute these differences to variation in firms' relationship capability.

  </div>
</details>

<small>*Presented at: IEA 21st World Congress (Belgrade), JIE Summer School in International
Economics (Milan, poster), ANETI Lab Seminar (Budapest)*</small>

> <span style="color: #004488">**Artificial Intelligence, Data and Creative Destruction**</span>  
<span class="emoji-link">📝</span><span style="color: #0066cc">*[Draft PDF](Artificial_Intelligence_Data.pdf)*</span>
<details class="abstract-toggle">
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
  <div class="abstract-body">

Artificial intelligence (AI) can codify tacit knowledge, but what it codifies inside a firm is that firm’s own knowledge. I build and calibrate an endogenous growth model in which firms implement the same technology to different degrees, governed by their accumulated data. Implementation substitutes capital for labour and relaxes the span of control, since tasks performed by AI need no oversight, so how efficiency scales with firm size becomes an equilibrium outcome. Because the return to implementation rises with data, which are lost on exit, AI favours large incumbents, makes them harder to displace and reduces the number of firms. Growth rises as incumbents expand, but weaker creative destruction bounds the gain: doubling AI’s potential raises growth by only 0.26 percentage points. Requiring firms to release their data after a few years turns them into public, accumulating knowledge and outperforms research and entry subsidies on welfare, at no fiscal cost.

  </div>
</details>
